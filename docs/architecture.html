ledger-core · Part 2

Architecture & Trade-offs

Yazan Tommalieh · November 2026 · Companion to the Part 1 repository

The core has one primitive: balance(account, value_date_upto, as_of_posting_day), a sum over an append-only log where every record carries the day it was posted and the day it is effective. There is no running balance. Hold state, the once-per-day fee check and the interest schedule are all queries over that log. That is why it is correct under backdated entries, and why it is slow.

1Append-only at scale

What breaks first: the end-of-day close

Every close re-checks every past day for every account, because a backdated entry can make an already-closed day negative. Each check scans the whole log. One close costs about days × accounts × entries, and entries grow with days × accounts: quadratic in both. At 100× accounts it does roughly 10,000× the work, and the close is the one job with a deadline. Nothing goes wrong; it just stops finishing on time.

Where state grows without bound

The three logs (entries, authorizations, errors) grow by design: they are the audit record. The set of seen event ids grows by accident. Every lookup is a linear scan, and one global sequence number couples all accounts, making the engine a single writer.

Cheapest fix: close the period

Pick a horizon H, the last closed business day. Refuse any entry value-dated before H - one comparison in append_entry. At each close, write one snapshot row per account: balance, holds and fee flags as of H.

Then every balance is snapshot plus a scan of entries after H; the close re-checks only the days since H; the id set only needs ids after H. Audit is unchanged because the snapshot is derived from the log. It bounds the problem instead of speeding up the scan.

Left for later: the log stays unbounded (five-year retention), and parallel processing needs a sequence per account. An index comes after those.

2Value-dated entries in production

A value-dated entry rewrites history. In Part 1 one backdated debit restated three closed days, produced three fees in one close and declined an authorization; its reversal restated the days again and left the fees standing. In a UAE-licensed bank each effect has an owner.

Customers and complaintsIssued statements become wrong, and a fee appears for a day the customer saw as positive. CBUAE Consumer Protection Standards (Circular 8/2020, Art. 8): acknowledge a complaint within 2 business days, answer within 30, keep the file five years. Re-assessed fees will be the main source of those complaints.
FeesOverdraft charges sit under the CBUAE retail fee caps (Regulations 29/2011, Annexure 2, amended 2018); a new fee or a change above 5% needs prior CBUAE approval. An engine that turns one backdated debit into three charges is a fee behaviour compliance must approve and the customer agreement must describe.
FinanceInterest is recomputed from restated history, so a backdated entry across a month-end changes reported income; the general ledger needs reversing journals the core does not produce.
Audit and AMLEvery record has both dates, so “what did we know on Day 5” is a query, and the log meets the five-year retention in Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025. It does not record who backdated an entry or why.
ContextUnder EU PSD2 Art. 87(3) a debit cannot be value-dated earlier than the debit itself, so the Part 1 backdated debit would be unlawful there as a customer payment. In a UAE bank, legitimate backdating is bank-side only: scheme clearing (T+1/T+2, carrying the transaction date), corrections and adjustments.

The one control before go-live: a backdating horizon with maker-checker

The core refuses entries value-dated before the last closed day. Anything that must land behind it goes into an adjustments queue; a second person approves it with a reason code, and the approval is appended to the log.

This bounds how much history can be rewritten, creates the audit trail an examiner asks for first, and is the condition under which automatic fee re-assessment is safe. The second control - linking each fee to the entry that caused it, so reversing the cause reverses the fee - is the failing test in Part 1 and item 4 in section 4.

3Authorization lifecycle

In the model an authorization is a decision record whose state (declined, active, settled) is derived from whether a settlement references it. The only ending implemented in full is a matching settlement. The others:

EndingReal-world caseWhat I would mandate
Declined at requestToday: recorded, no hold, never revisitedNot enough available funds (in practice also fraud rules).Final, with a reason code; a later backdated credit does not re-open it.
Duplicate requestToday: rejected as an errorNetwork retry.Return the original decision, no second hold.
Never settledToday: the hold lives foreverAbandoned pre-authorization - hotel, car rental, fuel, a cancelled order.Expire per scheme rules - Visa 7 days (31 for lodging, car rental, cruise), Mastercard 7 (30 for pre-authorizations); release the hold, record the expiry.
Reversed by the merchantToday: no such eventOrder cancelled before capture.A new event; full reversal voids the hold, partial reduces it; only the merchant can originate it.
Late presentment or no authorizationToday: rejected (Part 1 criterion 4)Stand-in processing while the issuer was down, offline chip approval, force-post.Post it, flagged, so the ledger matches money the scheme has already settled, and send it to disputes; the remedy is a chargeback, not refusal.
Settled for a different amountToday: partial releases the whole hold; over-settlement posts with a warningFinal bill below the estimate; tips above it.Partial releases the remainder unless multi-capture was flagged; over-capture within the scheme's tolerance for that merchant type.

4What I cut and why

Sixteen simplifications, grouped. Each row: what was cut, why it stayed out of scope, and the production risk it defers.

In one line: the core is a query over an immutable log - right and slow. Production keeps the log and adds a horizon, links on derived entries, and the authorization events the card world actually sends.
