ledger-core · Part 2

Architecture & Trade-offs

Yazan Tommalieh · November 2026 · Companion to the Part 1 repository

The core has one primitive: balance(account, value_date_upto, as_of_posting_day), a sum over an append-only log where every record carries the day it was posted and the day it is effective. There is no running balance; holds, fees and interest are all queries over the log. That is why it is correct under backdated entries, and why it is slow.

1Append-only at scale

What breaks first: the end-of-day close

Every close re-checks every past day for every account, because a backdated entry can make a closed day negative, and each check scans the whole log. Cost per close is about days × accounts × entries, and entries grow with days × accounts: quadratic in both. At 100× accounts that is roughly 10,000× the work against a fixed deadline. Nothing goes wrong; it just stops finishing on time.

Where state grows without bound

The three logs (entries, authorizations, errors) grow by design: they are the audit record. The set of seen event ids grows by accident. Every lookup is a linear scan, and one global sequence number makes the engine a single writer.

Cheapest fix: close the period

Pick a horizon H, the last closed business day. Refuse entries value-dated before H (one comparison in append_entry) and, at each close, snapshot every account's balance, holds and fee flags as of H. A balance is then snapshot plus a scan of entries after H, and the close re-checks only the days since H. Audit is unchanged because the snapshot derives from the log. Still open afterwards: the log itself (five-year retention) and per-account sequences for parallelism.

2Value-dated entries in production

A value-dated entry rewrites history. In Part 1 one backdated debit restated three closed days, produced three fees in one close and declined an authorization; its reversal restated the days again and left the fees standing. In a UAE-licensed bank each effect has an owner.

Customers and complaintsIssued statements become wrong and fees appear for days the customer saw as positive. CBUAE Consumer Protection Standards (Circular 8/2020, Art. 8): acknowledge a complaint in 2 business days, answer in 30, keep the file five years. Re-assessed fees will be the main source of those complaints. (EU PSD2 Art. 87(3) forbids a debit value date earlier than the debit itself; here, legitimate backdating is bank-side only: scheme clearing, corrections, adjustments.)
FeesOverdraft charges sit under the CBUAE retail fee caps (Regulations 29/2011, Annexure 2); a new fee or a change above 5% needs prior approval. One backdated debit becoming three charges is a fee behaviour compliance must sign off and the customer agreement must describe.
FinanceInterest is recomputed from restated history, so a backdated entry across a month-end changes reported income and needs reversing journals the core does not produce.
Audit and AMLBoth dates on every record make “what did we know on Day 5” a query and meet the five-year retention in Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025. Missing: who backdated an entry, and why.

The one control before go-live: a backdating horizon with maker-checker

The core refuses entries value-dated before the last closed day. Anything that must land behind it goes into an adjustments queue; a second person approves it with a reason code, and the approval is appended to the log. This bounds how much history can be rewritten, gives the examiner the who-and-why trail, and makes automatic fee re-assessment safe. The second control, linking each fee to its trigger so reversing the cause reverses the fee, is the failing test in Part 1 (Fees, below).

3Authorization lifecycle

An authorization is a decision record; its state (declined, active, settled) is derived from whether a settlement references it. The only ending implemented in full is a matching settlement. The others:

4What I cut and why

Storage and scale
In-memory lists, linear scans, one global sequence (persistence out of scope)
Quadratic close; single writer; a crash loses the idempotency set, so a replay can double-post.
Backdating unbounded (the exercise depends on it)
Full-history restatement; covered by the horizon in section 2.
Money model
Single-sided entries, no double-entry, no transfers (none in the stream)
No debits-equal-credits check; nothing reconciles to the general ledger.
Simple daily interest, positive only, one capitalisation; no debit interest, tiers or Islamic profit model (spec wording)
Wrong for any real product; a restated accrual across a reporting period has no journal.
HALF_UP, rounded daily then summed (spec wording)
Must match the customer agreement; the difference is fils.
Only an AED fee configured (the spec gives one)
An overdrawn BHD account raises an error instead of a charge.
Fees
No link from a fee to its trigger, no fee reversal (spec never says when to unwind; the failing test)
A reversed bank error leaves the customer paying. Fix: a trigger reference on each derived entry and a compensating fee reversal.
Debits never declined, no overdraft limit, flat fee with no cap (spec gives none)
Unlimited overdraft and fee stacking; needs a limit per account and a cap per cycle.
Authorizations
No hold expiry, merchant reversal, incremental authorization or multi-capture (none in the stream)
Holds lock funds forever; a merchant void, the most common real ending, cannot be represented.
Approvals never revisited after a backdated debit (how card networks work)
Exposure sits with the bank; nothing flags “approved on a balance later restated”.
No account lifecycle (out of scope)
Freeze and closure behaviour for holds is undefined.
Time
Integer days, no calendar (the brief's days are abstract)
Weekends, holidays, cut-offs and Gulf-time day boundaries are undefined.
Future value dates refused (keeps “balance as of day d” unambiguous)
Standing and scheduled payments cannot be represented.
Input and errors
Structural faults stop the replay (right for a test harness)
One bad message halts production; needs a dead-letter queue.
Rejections are only logged (the brief asks for errors to be printed)
A real force-post never reaches disputes; settled money goes unaccounted.
No operator, channel, reason code or reference on entries (none in the stream)
Audit knows what and when, not who or why; no reconciliation to scheme files.

In one line: a query over an immutable log, right and slow. Production keeps the log and adds a horizon, links on derived entries, and the authorization events the card world actually sends.
